# Architecture: the files and what they contain

`PM.lean` imports `PM/Vol1.lean`, the entry file for Volume I (Vols II and III will get their own). The
design notes give the reasons for the choices summarized here: [STAR9_DESIGN.md](STAR9_DESIGN.md) for
✱9–✱11 and [TYPES_DESIGN.md](TYPES_DESIGN.md) for ✱12 onward, each choice with the 1910 page it rests on.

## ✱1–✱5: propositional logic

- `PM/Basic.lean`: formulas built from variables, `∼` and `∨`. The definitions ✱1·01, ✱3·01 and ✱4·01 are
  macros. `Thm` is exactly PM's primitive propositions ✱1·1–✱1·6. The file also proves soundness and gives
  a counter-model test.
- `PM/Statements.lean`: the statement of every ✱2–✱5 proposition, as read from the 1910 printed scan, each
  checked to be a tautology by `decide`. It is generated from `data/vol1.jsonl` by `scripts/gen_lean.py`,
  with ✱1·4 corrected to the print ([scripts/README.md](../scripts/README.md)).
- `PM/Replay.lean`: 103 propositions replayed as explicit `Thm` terms built from PM's own cited
  propositions. 72 use only the propositions PM cites; 31 also need at least one proposition PM does not
  cite, each recorded in `data/replay_ledger.json`.
- `PM/Derived.lean` and `PM/Replay2.lean`: derived rules (syllogism, ∧/≡ introduction and elimination, a
  deduction theorem) and the remaining 85 propositions. 74 of the 85 follow PM's own proof
  ([FIDELITY.md](FIDELITY.md)).
- `PM/Complete.lean`: a Kalmár-style completeness theorem, `complete : p.isTaut = true → Thm p`. No replay
  depends on it.
- `PM/Star2.lean`: the one-letter difference between the Gutenberg #78050 transcription of ✱1·4 (`p ∨ p`)
  and the 1910 print (`q ∨ p`): the variant is not a theorem, which is how the slip was found.

## ✱9–✱11: apparent variables

This part follows ✱10's "alternative method" (PM pp. 143–144): `∼`, `∨` and `(x)` are primitive for formulas
with or without apparent variables, and ✱1's primitive propositions hold for all of them.

- `PM/Quant.lean`: formulas `QF V` over a family `V : Ty → Type` of variables, one type of variables per type
  of argument.
  - The function φx̂ with an argument of type τ is a Lean function `V τ → QF V`, so there is no
    substitution and no variable capture. ✱10·01–✱10·03 are macros.
  - `QThm` is ✱1·1–✱1·6 for all formulas, plus ✱10·1, ✱10·11 (a rule) and ✱10·12, at every type, plus the
    rules of ✱12 described below.
  - The file proves soundness (`qsound`). `liftThm` carries any ✱1–✱5 theorem over to `QF`.
- `PM/QuantLift.lean`: all 188 ✱2–✱5 propositions restated for `QF` (generated by
  `scripts/gen_quant_lift.py`).
- `PM/Star10.lean`: ✱9·1, ✱9·11, ✱9·12, ✱9·13, ✱9·2, ✱9·21–✱9·25, and ✱10·1–✱10·57 (50 propositions).
  ✱10·121, ✱10·122 and ✱10·221 concern significance and are built into the syntax. The proofs follow PM's
  lines; each docstring names the steps PM leaves tacit. Each theorem holds at every type of its apparent
  variable.
- `PM/Found9/` (namespace `PM.F9`): ✱9 by its own method, beside the foundation above and changing none of
  it ([STAR9_FOUNDATION.md](STAR9_FOUNDATION.md)). Elementary formulas `EF` and ✱9 propositions `PF`, with
  ∼ and ∨ of quantified propositions computed by ✱9·01–✱9·08: as printed (`PF.orPrinted`), in Landini's
  ✱9+ with ✱9·yy and ✱9·zz (`PF.orPlus`), and by an alternative convention (`PF.or`) (`Syntax.lean`);
  ✱9's primitive propositions as the inductive `Thm9 S R`, for every `Sys` and every reading `R`: A, as
  printed; C, as printed + ✱8·13, the printed definitions with Russell's rule ✱8·13 of the second
  edition carried over, the working reading and the default; L, Landini's ✱9+ with his rule Switch, a
  published variant and cross-check; B, the alternative convention, a cross-check (`System.lean`,
  `Switch.lean`, `Exch.lean`; [REPAIRS.md](REPAIRS.md)); ✱9·2–✱9·52 along PM's lines, for every reading
  except ✱9·31, which is proved in C (with ✱8·13), in L (with Switch) and under B and open under A, and
  ✱10·01, ✱10·1, ✱10·11, ✱10·12 in ✱9 (`Base.lean`, `Star9.lean`); p. 142 for one apparent variable per
  letter, 50, 65, 63 and 67 of 70 cases under A, C, L and B (`Simul.lean`, with the search's meta code in
  `SearchTactic.lean`;
  [STAR9_READINGS.md](STAR9_READINGS.md)); the agreement with ✱10's method, `star9_iff_star10`, with ✱9's soundness and its
  failure without an individual (`ToStar10.lean`, `FromStar10.lean`, `Equiv.lean`). The agreement
  assumes a variable of every type (`hV`) and ✱1·2–✱1·6 for every ✱9 proposition (`Rep`, PM's claim on
  p. 142, not proved); every ✱9 theorem is a ✱10 theorem given `hV` alone.
- `PM/Chain.lean`: PM's displayed chains as Lean `calc` blocks.
  - `A ≡ B` and `A ⊃ B` are asserted lines; `A ≡[H] B` and `A ⊃[H] B` are lines under the hypothesis H
    (PM's "Hp . ⊃ : A . ≡ . B . [✱x] ≡ . C").
  - The `Trans` instances that join two lines are proved once, from PM's propositions: Syll and ✱4·22 for
    asserted lines, and under a hypothesis also Comp (✱3·43), ✱2·02 and ✱2·83.
  - The header gives PM's warrant for leaving the joining step tacit (Introduction p. 9, the commentary on
    ✱2·31 on pp. 109–110, p. 123). ✱11·71, ✱13, ✱14 and ✱20 write their chains this way, one `calc` line
    per PM line with PM's citation in a comment. ✱21 prints only bracketed hints, so it has no chains.
- `PM/Star11.lean`: ✱11 (pp. 157–167). The definitions ✱11·01–✱11·06 are macros.
  - All 51 numbered assertions are proved (✱11·1–✱11·71, including ✱11·521 and ✱11·53), and so are the
    four numbered rules ✱11·07, ✱11·11, ✱11·13 and ✱11·311.
  - PM takes ✱11·07 (interchanging x and y) as a primitive proposition; here it is derived from ✱11·1 and
    ✱10·11, so no primitive is added.
  - The two apparent variables may have different types.
  - As in ✱10, propositions whose proofs pass through ✱10·33, ✱10·34 or ✱10·37 take real variables
    (✱11·43, ✱11·46, ✱11·47, ✱11·56, ✱11·57, ✱11·59, ✱11·61).
  - The file also proves that the converse of ✱11·26 is not a theorem (PM p. 161).

## ✱12 onward: ramified types and reducibility (1910)

- *Types.* `Ty` (in `PM/Quant.lean`) has individuals and the ramified types of functions of one and of two
  arguments.
  - `fn1 a k` has order `a.ord + 1 + k` (Introduction p. 56). The predicative functions φ!x̂ of an `a` have
    the type `pred1 a = fn1 a 0`, the lowest order compatible with the argument.
  - Arguments may be of any type and order (p. 174). There is no type of propositions (p. 172).
  - Apparent function variables are applied by `app1` and `app2`. PM's real function variables of
    unassigned order (φx̂, p. 173) are Lean functions `V a → QF V`, as in ✱9–✱11.
- *Orders.* `Lvl p n` is the order condition, with a real function variable at its own order (p. 55).
  `LvlArg` is the alternative reading, in which it counts as an argument (p. 54;
  `PM/Pred/RealAsArgument.lean`, also decided at `U`). `PM/Orders.lean` decides the condition for formulas
  over the one-element variable family `U` only, and checks 32 order facts taken from the Introduction
  (pp. 54–56) and ✱12 (pp. 170–171). See [SEMANTICS.md](SEMANTICS.md) for what this does not cover.
- *Rules as opt-in modules.* The proof system is `QThm S`, for a system `S : Sys` that says which rules
  beyond ✱1–✱11 it has and its style of predicativity. Each rule is its own module with its PM warrant in
  the header:
  - `PM/Rules/Reducibility.lean`: ✱12·1 `red1` and ✱12·11 `red2` (p. 174), for φ of any order and f
    predicative; class `Reducibility S`.
  - `PM/Rules/Comprehension.lean`: a formula the style admits at a function type is formally equivalent to
    a function variable of that type. This is our reading of ✱9·15/✱10·122, marked for what it is for;
    class `Comprehension S`. No numbered PM proposition uses it.
  - Lean enforces that a rule is enabled in the system: its constructors need a proof of `S.red = true`
    (resp. `S.compr = true`). A theorem using a rule gets this proof from the class in its signature, so
    the signature shows the dependency; that is the repository's convention, which `RedCheck.lean` checks
    ([VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md)). A theorem that uses no rule holds in every system.
  - `PM/Systems.lean` names four systems: `Sys.full`, `Sys.reducibility`, `Sys.predicative`, `Sys.base`.
- *Styles of predicativity* (`PM/Pred/`). PM defines "predicative" twice: as a matrix, a function with no
  apparent variables (✱12 p. 172), and as the lowest order compatible with the arguments (Introduction
  p. 56). It also allows two readings of a real (free) function variable in the order count: at its own
  order (p. 55) or as an argument (p. 54).
  - Each reading is its own module, and the system takes the style as a parameter: `Style.of P R` combines
    a reading of "predicative" (`Matrix.lean`, the primary style: comprehension at a predicative type
    accepts only matrices, and only of the right order; `LowestOrder.lean`, the alternative) with a reading
    of real variables (`RealOwnOrder.lean`, the default; `RealAsArgument.lean`): four styles in all.
  - They differ only in which direct applications of comprehension they accept (its side condition, a
    syntactic fact). `PM/Pred/Styles.lean` has golden tests of where that differs (e.g. (x) . φ!x as a
    function of φ, p. 171: `p171_compr_lowestOrder`, `p171_compr_matrix`; φ!x with φ real, pp. 54–55:
    `realPhi_compr_ownOrder`, `realPhi_compr_asArgument`) and proves that the matrix style admits no more
    than the lowest-order one.
  - Every PM theorem is stated for an arbitrary system, so for every style; `StyleCheck.lean` instantiates
    each under each style and system. Consistency is proved for every style. No proposition is proved
    here to hold in some styles and not in others.
  - A refused comprehension application does not make a proposition underivable: (∃g) : φ!x ≡ₓ g!x is
    refused to comprehension in the p. 54 styles, yet it is a theorem of every system, since φ itself is a
    witness (`realPhi_every_system`).
  - `p171_lowestOrder_predicative` derives p. 171 (1)'s existential in the lowest-order predicative system
    by the direct application; whether it is derivable in the matrix-style predicative fragment is not
    proved either way (test F4).
- *Styles agree under reducibility.* `styles_equiv` (`PM/StylesEquiv.lean`): with ✱12·1 and ✱12·11, any
  two styles derive exactly the same theorems, with or without comprehension. It is a kernel-checked
  induction over derivations, uses no axioms, and uses ✱12·1/✱12·11 (it takes `[Reducibility S]`; RedCheck
  shows it reaches the rule). Without reducibility the matrix style admits less; that it then proves
  strictly less is not proved here (it needs a model that sees orders).
- *Partial application and p. 56's second clause* (`PM/Pred/Curry.lean`).
  - `curry_order`: the order of a body as a function of two arguments is the larger of the first
    argument's lowest order and the order of the partial application with that argument fixed. So φ!x is of
    order 2 as a function of (φ, x) (pp. 54, 170) and predicative as a function of x with φ fixed (p. 55).
  - p. 56's second clause ("if there is one of its arguments such that, when the other arguments have
    values assigned to them, we obtain a predicative function of the one undetermined argument") agrees
    with the lowest-order rule within this encoding (`second_clause_iff_lowestOrder`, and for any number
    of arguments `anyLe_iff_le_maxL`). "Assigning values" to the other arguments is evaluating both partial
    applications at `()` in the one-element family `U`, so both have the same body order O, and the
    equivalence reduces to the arithmetic O ≤ a + 1 ∨ O ≤ b + 1 ↔ O ≤ max a b + 1.
  - `ClauseSearch.lean` compares the same definitions on 2,784,780 cases and finds no disagreement. This
    does not independently show that the encoding captures PM's "assignment of values", nor extend to
    arbitrary variable families.
- `PM/Star12.lean`: ✱12·1 and ✱12·11, stated with PM's macros, and a check that the variable form of ✱12·1
  plus comprehension gives the formula form without `red1`.
- `PM/Star13.lean`: identity. ✱13·01–✱13·03 are macros. All 25 numbered propositions ✱13·1–✱13·3 are
  proved. ✱13·101 follows PM's proof through ✱12·1. ✱13·3 (p. 180) is about significance; its proof's two
  uses of ✱10·221 identify the types of two variables, which here is the Lean typing (x and a have one
  type).
- `PM/Star14.lean`: descriptions. ✱14·01–✱14·04 are macros that take the descriptions' scopes explicitly.
  - PM's conventions are applied when a statement is written: an unmarked description has the smallest
    scope, and of two descriptions the one written first has the larger (p. 182).
  - All 47 numbered propositions ✱14·1–✱14·34 are proved; ✱14·17 and ✱14·171 cite ✱12·1, as PM's proofs
    do.
  - ✱14·3 (p. 194) is proved as a schema, not as printed. As printed it takes propositions p, q as apparent
    variables (and f is a function of propositions), and these types have none (p. 172). `q14_3` takes its
    antecedent p ≡ q . ⊃_{p,q} . f(p) ≡ f(q), with p, q real, as a premise proved for every p, q, so it is
    one theorem per f, φ, χ, not the quantified proposition. `qtfnCongr` proves the premise for every
    truth-function built by ✱1–✱11's processes (p. 193). PM itself proves ✱14·31–✱14·34 without ✱14·3,
    and so do the proofs here.
  - ✱14·32 is proved as printed, with "≡" after E!(ιx)(φx): its hint gives the "⊃" half, and the converse,
    which pp. 194–195 explain, takes ✱14·21 for both descriptions (✱3·27, ✱2·54, ✱3·44; recorded in the
    ledger).
  - Where the print is unclear the docstring says so: ✱14·144 and ✱14·145 cite ✱14·111 where the unmarked
    form is ✱14·112; ✱14·272 cites ✱10·414 for a ✱10·271 step; ✱14·16 and ✱14·203 cite "✱10·1·23" and
    "✱10·1·28" for ✱10·11·23 and ✱10·11·28; ✱14·28's Dem. eliminates (ιx)(φx) = (ιx)(φx) as one
    description.
  - The file also proves that when the negation has the wider scope (the description the narrower),
    ∼{[(ιx)(φx)] . χ(ιx)(φx)} does not imply E!(ιx)(φx).
- `PM/Star20.lean`: classes. ✱20·01, ✱20·02, ✱20·03, ✱20·04–✱20·081 are macros.
  - Identity of classes is ✱13·01 at the type of predicative functions, so ✱13 is used there at a higher
    type (typical ambiguity as Lean parameters).
  - A class is written by its defining function: a real Greek letter by any formula with a hole (p. 203),
    an apparent one by φ!ẑ with φ bound (✱20·07, p. 198). Each occurrence of a class is eliminated where
    it stands, with ✱14's scope conventions (p. 197) and descriptions wider than classes (p. 204).
  - 53 of the 55 numbered propositions ✱20·1–✱20·81 are proved, ✱20·62 and ✱20·633 as rules. ✱20·631 and
    ✱20·632 concern significance and are built into the syntax, like ✱10·121 and ✱10·122.
  - Classes of classes (✱20·03, ✱20·08, ✱20·081) are built as printed, one named lemma per layer of
    elimination, and ✱20·4, ✱20·41 (α ∈ Cls) use the analogue of ✱20·3 for classes of classes
    (`qcls20_3`, p. 206) ([CLASSES_OF_CLASSES.md](CLASSES_OF_CLASSES.md)). `x ∈ α`, `α ∈ κ` are notation
    scoped to `PM`. The extensionality of a class expression, which `qcls20_3` takes as a premise, is
    proved for every expression PM's notation forms (`ClsExpr`, `clsExpr_ext`; p. 200).
  - Where PM instantiates an apparent class at a class ẑ(φz) that need not be predicative, citing ✱10·1 or
    ✱10·24 (✱20·25, ✱20·53, ✱20·54), the step is the class analogue p. 206 points to (✱20·17's argument:
    ✱12·1 and ✱20·11).
  - ✱20·59's Dem. eliminates the class before the description, against p. 204's convention; the statement
    follows the convention (docstring).
- `PM/Star21.lean`: relations, as ✱20 for classes ("exactly analogous … merely substituting ✱12·11 for
  ✱12·1, and propositions in ✱11 for propositions in ✱10", p. 212). ✱21·01, ✱21·02, ✱21·03,
  ✱21·07–✱21·083 are macros.
  - 46 of the 48 numbered propositions ✱21·1–✱21·71 are proved, ✱21·62 and ✱21·633 as rules; ✱21·631,
    ✱21·632 are built into the syntax.
  - Relations of relations and classes of relations (✱21·08–✱21·083) are built as in ✱20, and ✱21·4,
    ✱21·41 (R ∈ Rel) use the analogue of ✱20·3 for classes of relations (`qrelCls20_3`).
  - ✱21·57 cites ✱13·183, whose analogue for relations is not printed in ✱21; it is proved by ✱20·25's
    argument.
- `PM/Trap.lean`: two degenerate variable families at which every formula is a theorem
  ([SEMANTICS.md](SEMANTICS.md)), with ✱12·1, and at the one-element family also with comprehension alone
  (`trap_unit_compr`).

## Models

- `PM/Models/Collapse.lean`: the collapse model, every type of functions a full function space, with
  classical quantifiers; with reducibility, a model of the 1910 system. Its general form uses
  `Classical.choice` ([VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md)).
- `PM/Models/Finite.lean`: the finite coded model, with truth tables and quantifiers by enumeration.
  `consistent`, `consistent_all` and the five counter-models are proved there without axioms.

## Checks, scripts and data

- Root check files: `AxiomCheck.lean`, `AllAxioms.lean`, `RedCheck.lean`, `StyleCheck.lean`,
  `ClauseSearch.lean` ([VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md)).
- `Checks/`: `PinnedStatements.lean` (part of `lake build`: the exact statements of propositions whose
  statement was once corrected), `LedgerCheck.lean` (citations and ledger rows against proof terms) and
  `Star9Readings.lean` (✱9 under its four readings, read from the statements, and where C uses ✱8·13 and
  L uses Switch;
  docs/STAR9_READINGS.md) and `ExportStatements.lean` (each PM proposition's statement as a tree, from
  which `scripts/export_names.py` generates export names; [NAMING.md](NAMING.md)).
- `tests/`: the regression tests and `run_all.sh`, which runs every check
  ([tests/README.md](../tests/README.md)).
- `scripts/`: the statement parser and generators, and the progress counter
  ([scripts/README.md](../scripts/README.md)). `scripts/mutation/` is the statement-mutation trial, a Lean
  meta-program and its driver ([STATEMENT_MUTATION.md](STATEMENT_MUTATION.md)).
- `data/`: the fidelity ledgers, the parsed Vol I text and the item lists
  ([data/README.md](../data/README.md)).

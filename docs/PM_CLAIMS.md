# PM's claims about its own system

*Principia Mathematica* often says something about its own system rather than in it: that two methods are
alternatives, that proofs carry over "by merely repeating previous proofs", that a result "can be proved in
each particular case", that a notation is always eliminable, that a formula is not a theorem. This project
treats each such assertion as a claim to check in Lean, not an assumption to adopt
([PRINCIPLES.md](PRINCIPLES.md), principle 5). This page lists them, with their status.

**Scope.** The Introduction and ✱1–✱21 of Vol. I (1910), pp. 1–216, read in full for this list. Claims that
merely restate a numbered proposition proved in the body are not listed one by one (row F3).

**Quotations** are PM's words, checked against Project Gutenberg eBook #78050 (the transcription this
repository's statements come from, [scripts/README.md](../scripts/README.md)); pages are those of the 1910
print. Formulas are written in PM's notation; "…" marks an omission.

**Status:**
- **checked**: a Lean theorem, check or construction establishes the claim (the Lean column says which);
- **partly checked**: established for part of what PM claims; the column says which part, and what is open;
- **assumed**: built into the formalization rather than proved; the column says how and why;
- **in progress**: work has started that will check it;
- **not attempted**: no Lean work yet; the column says what it would need where that is known.

"✱9's method" and "✱10's method" are PM's two ways of founding the theory of apparent variables (p. 144).
This repository is built on ✱10's ([STAR9_DESIGN.md](STAR9_DESIGN.md)). A parallel foundation built from
✱9's own primitive propositions is in progress, and is not yet in this repository.

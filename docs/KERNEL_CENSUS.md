# Kernel census: what of Lean's kernel the repository relies on

The proofs here are checked by Lean's kernel. This page answers which parts of that kernel they actually
use. Every kind of inductive type, the universe levels, definitional unfolding, literals and the kernel's
built-in arithmetic, quotients and axioms are counted, with the declaration that needs each one and the
chain of uses that reaches it. The answer scopes a restricted checker that accepts only what the PM
derivations need, and tells independent re-checks of the same proofs what they must support. All
figures are generated by `scripts/kernel_census/census.py`; nothing on this page is copied by hand from a
run.

```
python3 scripts/kernel_census/census.py --ratchet   # the static walk and the ratchet, about 15 s
python3 scripts/kernel_census/census.py --check     # the full census, about 80 s; fails if the tables
                                                    # below differ from it (--write rewrites them)
```

## In brief

These statements are read from the generated tables below. The ratchet keeps the ones about the
derivations true (see [The ratchet](#the-ratchet)).

- **No dependencies besides Lean.** `lake-manifest.json` lists no packages, so Mathlib is not involved.
  The closure is the repository plus Lean core.
- **To check the derivations and everything they use, a kernel needs** inductive families with indices
  (the proof systems `Thm`, `QThm` and `Thm9` are inductive predicates indexed by formulas), reflexive
  inductive types (the syntaxes `QF`, `PF` and `PE` bind variables by Lean functions), recursors with
  iota reduction, large elimination out of `Prop` (`Eq`, `And`, `False` …), the K-like recursors
  `Eq.rec`, `HEq.rec` and `True.rec`,
  structure-like types with primitive projections, universe polymorphism with `max` and `imax`,
  definitional unfolding of definitions with the hints `abbrev` and `regular`, `let`, Nat literals and the
  kernel's Nat operations on them, and String literals.
- **They do not need** mutual inductive types, quotients, any axiom, opaque constants, `partial` or
  `unsafe` declarations, or `Lean.ofReduceBool`, `Lean.ofReduceNat` and `Lean.trustCompiler`.
- **One nested inductive type, `Lean.Syntax`, reaches the derivations, by one route only.** The
  premise `n < Γ.length` of `PM.Pf.hy` (PM/Derived.lean) is an auto-param: the tactic that proves it is
  stored in the statement as a value of type `Lean.Syntax`, the declaration `PM.Pf.hy._auto_1`. That value
  brings `Lean.Syntax`, `Lean.Name` (whose constructors carry `@[implemented_by]`) and `Array` into the
  closure. It is the only declaration of the meta code (m) that any declaration of (a), (f) or (b) reaches.
- **Strings come with the formulas.** ✱1–✱5's variables are named by strings (`F.var "p"`). The type
  `String` is a structure over a byte array with a proof that the bytes are valid UTF-8, so its closure
  includes `ByteArray`, UTF-8 encoding, `UInt8`, `UInt32`, `BitVec` and `Nat.div`. When the kernel compares
  two string literals while checking a proof (as in ✱9's translations of ✱1–✱5 formulas, `PM.F9.e2_38`
  and its neighbours), it unfolds those definitions down to bytes.
- **The kernel computes.** Proofs by `rfl` and `decide` are checked by evaluation. The heaviest checks of
  derivations evaluate ✱9's definitions of ∼ and ∨ over quantified formulas by structural recursion
  (`PM.F9.PrenexSwitch.t1_6` and its neighbours). The `example`s of PM/Statements.lean evaluate
  the truth tables of ✱1–✱5 (`decide`). The auto-param of `PM.Pf.hy` computes `Nat.ble` on list lengths.
- **The kernel never unfolded a theorem or an opaque constant.** It did unfold definitions that the
  elaborator treats as `@[irreducible]` (`Nat.modCore`, in core lemmas), and the Lean definitions behind
  `@[extern]` (`Array.push`, `ByteArray.push`, `Char.ofNatAux`, `Nat.add` …), not the compiled code. No
  declaration anywhere in these closures names `Lean.ofReduceBool` or `Lean.ofReduceNat`, the kernel's
  only way to run compiled code.
- **Of the 15 Nat operations the kernel is said to compute on literals, the probes find 14 computed in
  Lean 4.34.1; `Nat.log2` is not** (its probe unfolds `Nat.log2`). The derivations' closure names `Nat.add`, `sub`,
  `mul`, `div`, `mod`, `beq`, `ble` and `pow`.
- **Axioms**: none in the closure of the derivations. The declarations that depend on one are those of
  `tests/expected_axiom_users.txt`, which [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md) describes, with the same
  axioms. Beyond them there are only internal auxiliaries of those declarations and the meta code (last
  table).

## What is counted

**The declarations of the repository** are those of the modules of the two library targets in
`lakefile.toml`: `PM` (every file under `PM/`) and `Checks` (its roots `Checks/PinnedStatements.lean` and
`Checks/Canon.lean`; the other files of `Checks/` are check programs run by `lake env lean`, not built).
They include what Lean generates for them (recursors, `casesOn`, matchers, equation lemmas, `injEq`, the
compiler's `_unsafe_rec` copies). Each module is in exactly one group. The lists are in
`scripts/kernel_census/KernelCensus.lean`, and a module in none of them stops the run.

- **(a) The PM derivations:** PM/Statements.lean, the replays (Replay, Replay2, Derived, QuantLift),
  ✱9–✱23 (Star10–Star23, Chain) and ✱9 by its own method (Found9/Base, Star9, Switch, Exch, Simul).
- **(f) The foundations:** the syntaxes and proof systems the derivations are built in (Basic, Quant,
  Rules/Reducibility, Rules/Comprehension, Found9/Syntax, Found9/System). These files also hold soundness
  theorems, which is why they are neither (a) nor (b).
- **(b) The metatheorems and models:** Complete, Star2, Systems, Orders, Trap, Pred/*, StylesEquiv,
  Models/*, Found9/ToStar10, FromStar10, Equiv, and Checks/PinnedStatements.lean.
- **(m) Meta code and notation:** Found9/SearchTactic.lean and Checks/Canon.lean, and, in any module, a
  declaration whose type names a constant of the `Lean` namespace: syntax, macros, elaborators and what Lean
  generates for them. (m) is counted separately. Its closure in core (the elaborator's own; the first
  line of the tables gives its size) is not tabled.
- **D, the derivation theorems:** every theorem of (a), (f) and (b) whose statement concludes `Thm …`,
  `QThm S …` or `Thm9 S R …`. That excludes definitions, notation and models that happen to sit in a
  derivation module. ✱11's counter-model, for one, is in PM/Star11.lean.
- **(c) Lean core:** every declaration outside the repository that a declaration of (a), (f) or (b) uses,
  directly or through others. A declaration *uses* the constants named in its type and value. An
  inductive type also uses its constructors, its recursor and the other types of its block, and a
  constructor or recursor uses its type, because the kernel adds them together.
- **The closure of (a)**, and **the closure of D**: everything they use, from the repository and from core.
  A checker of the derivations must check the closure of D.
- **The examples:** `example` commands are checked by the kernel during `lake build` but are not kept in
  the environment, so a census of the environment cannot see them. `census.py` copies each library file
  that has examples to `.lake/kernel_census/examples/`, renames each `example` to a theorem
  `_kernelCensusExample_<line>`, appends the census's functions and compiles the copy. The examples and their
  closure are two more columns. They are mostly computations: the truth-table checks of
  PM/Statements.lean, the order facts of PM/Orders.lean, and the pinned statements.

## How each row is measured

**Static (MEASURED).** `KernelCensus.lean` reads every declaration's `ConstantInfo` and every node of its
type and value, each shared subterm once. From the `ConstantInfo` it takes the kind, the universe
parameters, for an inductive type its indices, block, nesting, recursion and reflexivity and whether it is
structure-like, for a recursor whether it is K-like and whether a `Prop` type eliminates into every sort,
and for a definition its safety and reducibility hint. Lean's reducibility attribute, `@[implemented_by]`
and `@[extern]` come from the environment's attribute data. The kernel reads none of these three; they are
counted because they mark where the elaborator or compiler and the kernel see a definition differently.
From the nodes it takes the sorts and levels (`Prop`, `Type`, `Type 1` and higher, `Sort u`, `max`,
`imax`), Nat and String literals, primitive projections, `let`, and the constants named. *Large
elimination used* means a recursor of a `Prop` type applied at a motive universe other than `0`.
*K-like recursor named* means a K-like recursor occurs. A row counts the declarations of a column that
have the feature in their own type or value. The second static table gives, for each feature of the
closure of D, a shortest chain of uses from a derivation theorem.

**Dynamic (MEASURED).** Each theorem, definition and opaque constant of (a), (f), (b) and (c) is checked
again by the kernel, as a copy under a fresh name, with the kernel's diagnostics on (`Kernel.enableDiag`).
The kernel then records each constant it unfolds while checking (delta) and each recursor it reduces
(iota). This is the kernel's own record, not a reconstruction. Inductive types, constructors and
recursors are not re-checked, since a copy would need its whole block renamed; neither are `partial` or
`unsafe` definitions. All re-checks are accepted.

**Probes (MEASURED).** For each Nat operation the kernel is said to compute on literals, a theorem
`op a b = c` with literals of 130 to 400 bits is proved by `Eq.refl` and checked the same way. The
operation is computed on literals when the check succeeds without unfolding it.

**Not determinable by this census.** The kernel's diagnostics record delta and iota steps only, so these
uses cannot be seen, statically or from the record:

- definitional proof irrelevance (two proofs of one proposition identified);
- eta for functions, eta for structures, and the identification of the values of a unit-like type;
- whether a K-like recursor was reduced by K rather than by ordinary iota;
- the conversion between Nat literals and `Nat.succ`, and between a String literal and its constructor
  form;
- which steps the GMP-backed Nat operations took on literals (the probes show only that they are taken).

What can be said statically: every derivation is a proof (`Thm`, `QThm` and `Thm9` live in `Prop`), so
proof irrelevance *may* apply anywhere in them. The structure-like types and the K-like recursors in the
closure are listed, so the places where structure eta and K *may* apply are known. Counting the actual
uses needs an instrumented kernel. An independent checker such as lean4lean (roadmap lk-l4l) could count
them.

The dynamic part depends on the Lean version. The figures are for the toolchain named in
`lean-toolchain` and in the first line of the tables.

## What it implies for a restricted checker

The figures, read as requirements:

- **It must implement:** inductive families with indices; reflexive and recursive inductive types; the one
  nested type `Lean.Syntax`, as long as the auto-param of `PM.Pf.hy` stays (the only route to it);
  recursors with iota reduction and K; large elimination out of `Prop`; structure-like types and
  primitive projections; universe polymorphism with `max` and `imax`; `Prop`; structure eta and
  definitional proof irrelevance unless they are shown to be unused (this census cannot tell); delta
  with the hints `abbrev` and `regular`;
  `let`; Nat literals with the operations named in the closure of D; and String literals with their
  expansion into bytes.
- **It can omit:** mutual inductive types, quotients, axioms, opaque constants, `partial` and `unsafe`
  declarations, reduction by the compiler (`Lean.ofReduceBool`, `Lean.ofReduceNat`), and the
  accelerated `gcd`, `land`, `lor`, `xor`, `shiftLeft`, `shiftRight` and `log2`.
- **Its input is the closure of D.** The first line of the tables gives its size and how much of it is
  Lean core.
- **The examples are outside an export of the environment.** A checker that reads exported declarations
  sees the closure of D but not the examples, so not the truth-table checks of ✱1–✱5's statements.
  Those need either export as theorems or a check of their own.

## The ratchet

`tests/test_kernel_census.py` runs `census.py --ratchet`, which walks (a), (f) and (b) and their closures
(not the meta code) in about 15 s. It fails in two cases. The first is when the set of features of the
three columns *derivations*, *closure of (a)* and *closure of D* differs from
`tests/expected_kernel_features.txt`. A new feature is reported with its chain of uses, and a vanished
one is reported too. The second is when the declarations that depend on an axiom, excluding internal
auxiliaries, differ from `tests/expected_axiom_users.txt`. The test runs with the other Python tests in
`tests/run_all.sh`. To accept a new feature deliberately, run `census.py --ratchet --update` and say why
in the commit.

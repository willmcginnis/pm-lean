# Principia Mathematica ✱1–✱5, ✱9–✱14 (✱14·3 as a schema), ✱20 and ✱21 (all but ✱20·4·41, ✱21·4·41), in Lean 4

A deep embedding of the propositional part of Whitehead & Russell's *Principia Mathematica* (1910), of its theory of one and two apparent variables (✱9–✱11), and of its ramified types with the axiom of reducibility (✱12), with identity (✱13), descriptions (✱14) and the general theory of classes and relations (✱20, ✱21).

- `PM/Basic.lean`: formulas built from variables, `∼` and `∨`. The definitions ✱1·01, ✱3·01 and ✱4·01 are macros. `Thm` is exactly PM's primitive propositions ✱1·1–✱1·6. The file also proves soundness and gives a counter-model test.
- `PM/Statements.lean`: the statement of every ✱2–✱5 proposition, as read from the 1910 printed scan, and each checked to be a tautology by `decide`. It was generated by `data/gen_lean.py` from the companion text audit's proposition list and parser, which are not in this repository; the checked-in `PM/Statements.lean` is the artifact the proofs use, so building needs nothing else. (The script also writes to an older path, `lean/PM/Statements.lean`; adjust before rerunning.)
- `PM/Replay.lean`: 103 propositions replayed as explicit `Thm` terms built from PM's own cited propositions. 72 use only the propositions PM cites; 31 also need at least one proposition PM does not cite, and each addition is recorded in `data/replay_ledger.json`.
- `PM/Derived.lean` and `PM/Replay2.lean`: derived rules (syllogism, ∧/≡ introduction and elimination, a deduction theorem) and the remaining 85 propositions.
- `PM/Complete.lean`: a Kalmár-style completeness theorem, `complete : p.isTaut = true → Thm p`. No replay depends on it.
- `PM/Star2.lean`: checks a one-letter difference between the Gutenberg #78050 transcription of ✱1·4 (`p ∨ p`) and the 1910 print (`q ∨ p`): the variant is not a theorem, which is how the slip was found.

Status: all 188 ✱2–✱5 propositions are proved, all 51 numbered ✱11 assertions, ✱12·1 and ✱12·11 (primitive), all 25 numbered ✱13 propositions, all 47 numbered ✱14 propositions (✱14·3 as a schema, not as printed; see below), 51 of the 55 numbered ✱20 propositions and 44 of the 48 numbered ✱21 propositions (see below). Every PM theorem (✱2–✱5, ✱9–✱21) and `complete` reports "does not depend on any axioms" under `#print axioms`. The project contains no `sorry`, no `native_decide` and no added axioms.

An exhaustive check of all 1645 declarations under `PM` and the 31 private declarations of the PM modules (`AllAxioms.lean`) finds 25 that use Lean's standard axioms. None of them is a PM derivation, an axiom-free consistency theorem or a counter-model.

- *Compiler-generated* (`propext` only): 15 `injEq` lemmas, and a `Repr` instance and its function.
- *Propositional metatheorems* (`propext`, `Quot.sound`): `PM.sound`, `PM.not_thm_of_countermodel` and `PM.star1_4_variant_not_a_theorem`.
- *The general (classical) collapse model* (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`): `classicalAll`, its specification and `QModel.classical`, which give classical quantifiers over arbitrary function spaces, for any domain of individuals; and `consistent_collapse`, `consistent_collapse_of_style`, which evaluate in it. (The same consistency statements are proved without axioms in the finite model.)

The ✱9–✱20 soundness theorem `PM.qsound` uses no axioms, and neither do `consistent`, `consistent_all` and the five counter-models: they are proved in a finite coded model (`PM/Models/Finite.lean`), in which function values are truth tables and every quantifier is computed by enumeration.

Fidelity in one line (✱2–✱5, from the two ledgers): 112 proofs use only the propositions PM cites (72 in `Replay.lean`, 40 in `Replay2.lean`); 65 follow PM's proof but need at least one further proposition, each listed (31 + 34); 11 are not yet rewritten toward PM's lines.

`data/replay_ledger.json` and `data/replay2_ledger.json` record, for each proposition, which of PM's cited propositions the Lean proof uses.

In `PM/Replay2.lean`, 74 of the 85 proofs follow PM's own proof. Each has one Lean line per Dem. line, or, where PM prints only a bracketed hint, one per hinted proposition in PM's order. The only steps done silently are the ones PM itself leaves tacit; the file header lists them. The ledger's `fidelity` field is `pm-lines` for 40 proofs. It is `pm-lines+extra` for 34 proofs, which also need a proposition PM does not cite; `extra_used` and `reason` say which one and why. It is `unchanged` for 11 proofs whose transcription has no Dem. or hint to follow.

## ✱9–✱11: apparent variables

This part follows ✱10's "alternative method" (PM pp. 143–144): `∼`, `∨` and `(x)` are primitive for formulas with or without apparent variables, and ✱1's primitive propositions hold for all of them. The design note `docs/STAR9_DESIGN.md` compares this with ✱9's own method and a higher-order embedding.

- `PM/Quant.lean`: formulas `QF V` over a family `V : Ty → Type` of variables, one type of variables per type of argument. The function φx̂ with an argument of type τ is a Lean function `V τ → QF V`, so there is no substitution and no variable capture. ✱10·01–✱10·03 are macros. `QThm` is ✱1·1–✱1·6 for all formulas, plus ✱10·1, ✱10·11 (a rule) and ✱10·12, at every type, plus the rules of ✱12 described in the next section. The file proves soundness (`qsound`). The counter-models and consistency (`consistent`, stated at the variables of a model, not at an arbitrary `V`; see the scope note) are in `PM/Models/Finite.lean`. `liftThm` carries any ✱1–✱5 theorem over to `QF`.
- `PM/QuantLift.lean`: all 188 ✱2–✱5 propositions restated for `QF` (generated by `data/gen_quant_lift.py`).
- `PM/Star10.lean`: ✱9·1, ✱9·11, ✱9·12, ✱9·13, ✱9·2, ✱9·21–✱9·25, and ✱10·1–✱10·57 (50 propositions; ✱10·121, ✱10·122 and ✱10·221 concern significance and are built into the syntax). The proofs follow PM's lines; each docstring names the steps PM leaves tacit. Each theorem holds at every type of its apparent variable.
- `PM/Chain.lean`: PM's displayed chains as Lean `calc` blocks. `A ≡ B` and `A ⊃ B` are asserted lines, `A ≡[H] B` and `A ⊃[H] B` lines under the hypothesis H (PM's "Hp . ⊃ : A . ≡ . B . [✱x] ≡ . C"). The `Trans` instances that join two lines are proved once, from PM's propositions: Syll and ✱4·22 for asserted lines, and under a hypothesis also Comp (✱3·43), ✱2·02 and ✱2·83. The header gives PM's warrant for leaving the joining step tacit (Introduction p. 9, the commentary on ✱2·31 on pp. 109–110, p. 123). ✱11·71, ✱13 and ✱14 write their chains this way, one `calc` line per PM line with PM's citation in a comment.
- `PM/Star11.lean`: ✱11 (pp. 157–167). The definitions ✱11·01–✱11·06 are macros. All 51 numbered assertions are proved (✱11·1–✱11·71, including ✱11·521 and ✱11·53), and so are the four numbered rules ✱11·07, ✱11·11, ✱11·13 and ✱11·311. PM takes ✱11·07 (interchanging x and y) as a primitive proposition; here it is derived from ✱11·1 and ✱10·11, so no primitive is added. The two apparent variables may have different types. As in ✱10, propositions whose proofs pass through ✱10·33, ✱10·34 or ✱10·37 take real variables (✱11·43, ✱11·46, ✱11·47, ✱11·56, ✱11·57, ✱11·59, ✱11·61). The file also proves that the converse of ✱11·26 is not a theorem (PM p. 161).

Fidelity of ✱11, from `data/star11_ledger.json` (same fields as `data/replay2_ledger.json`): all 51 proofs follow PM's Dem. lines or bracketed hint. 34 are `pm-lines`: they use only the propositions PM cites, plus the steps PM leaves tacit throughout (listed in the file header). 17 are `pm-lines+extra`: they also need a proposition PM does not cite, and `extra_used` and `reason` say which one and why. Typical reasons: ✱10·33 or ✱10·35 used with the factors in the other order (✱4·3), double negations PM drops silently, and three hints that cite a proposition that does not give the step (✱11·341, ✱11·371, ✱11·6; see their docstrings). There is no per-proposition ledger for ✱9–✱10.

## ✱12–✱20: ramified types and reducibility (1910)

This part follows the 1910 edition: ramified types with the axiom of reducibility. The design note `docs/TYPES_DESIGN.md` gives each formal choice with the 1910 page it rests on and the alternative rejected, and the results of the acceptance tests (consistency, usability, faithfulness).

- *Types.* `Ty` (in `PM/Quant.lean`) has individuals and the ramified types of functions of one and of two arguments. `fn1 a k` has order `a.ord + 1 + k` (Introduction p. 56). The predicative functions φ!x̂ of an `a` have the type `pred1 a = fn1 a 0`, the lowest order compatible with the argument. Arguments may be of any type and order (p. 174). There is no type of propositions (p. 172). Apparent function variables are applied by `app1` and `app2`. PM's real function variables of unassigned order (φx̂, p. 173) are Lean functions `V a → QF V`, as in ✱9–✱11.
- *Orders.* `Lvl p n` is the order condition, with a real function variable at its own order (p. 55); `LvlArg` is the alternative reading in which it counts as an argument (p. 54; `PM/Pred/RealAsArgument.lean`, also decided at `U`). `PM/Orders.lean` decides it for formulas over the one-element variable family `U` only (there is no theorem transferring the result to an arbitrary `V`), and checks 32 order facts taken from the Introduction (pp. 54–56) and ✱12 (pp. 170–171).
- *Rules as opt-in modules.* The proof system is `QThm S`, for a system `S : Sys` that says which rules beyond ✱1–✱11 it has and its style of predicativity. Each rule is its own module with its PM warrant in the header: `PM/Rules/Reducibility.lean` (✱12·1 `red1` and ✱12·11 `red2`, p. 174, for φ of any order and f predicative; class `Reducibility S`) and `PM/Rules/Comprehension.lean` (a formula the style admits at a function type is formally equivalent to a function variable of that type; our reading of ✱9·15/✱10·122, marked for what it is for; class `Comprehension S`). Lean enforces that a rule is enabled in the system: its constructors need a proof of `S.red = true` (resp. `S.compr = true`). That a theorem using a rule gets this proof from the class in its signature, so that the signature shows the dependency, is the repository's convention; `RedCheck.lean` checks it, including evidence passed in any other way (such as an explicit hypothesis `h : S.red = true`). A theorem that uses no rule holds in every system. `PM/Systems.lean` names four systems (`Sys.full`, `Sys.reducibility`, `Sys.predicative`, `Sys.base`). `PM/Vol1.lean` is the entry file for Volume I (`PM.lean` imports it; Vol. II and III will get their own). `RedCheck.lean` cross-checks the signatures against the proofs (see the table below).
- *Styles of predicativity.* PM defines "predicative" twice: as a matrix, a function with no apparent variables (✱12 p. 172), and as the lowest order compatible with the arguments (Introduction p. 56). It also allows two readings of a real (free) function variable in the order count: at its own order (p. 55) or as an argument (p. 54). Each reading is its own module under `PM/Pred/`, and the system takes the style as a parameter: `Style.of P R` combines a reading of "predicative" (`Matrix.lean`, the primary style: comprehension at a predicative type accepts only matrices, and only of the right order; `LowestOrder.lean`, the alternative) with a reading of real variables (`RealOwnOrder.lean`, the default; `RealAsArgument.lean`), four styles in all. They differ only in which direct applications of comprehension they accept (its side condition, a syntactic fact). `PM/Pred/Styles.lean` has golden tests of where that differs (e.g. (x) . φ!x as a function of φ, p. 171: `p171_compr_lowestOrder`, `p171_compr_matrix`; φ!x with φ real, pp. 54–55: `realPhi_compr_ownOrder`, `realPhi_compr_asArgument`) and proves that the matrix style admits no more than the lowest-order one. Every PM theorem is stated for an arbitrary system, so for every style, and `StyleCheck.lean` instantiates each one under each style and system: all 481 PM propositions pass in `Sys.full` of all four styles. Consistency is proved for every style. No proposition is proved here to hold in some styles and not in others. A refused comprehension application does not make a proposition underivable: (∃g) : φ!x ≡ₓ g!x is refused to comprehension in the p. 54 styles, yet it is a theorem of every system, since φ itself is a witness (`realPhi_every_system`). `p171_lowestOrder_predicative` derives p. 171 (1)'s existential in the lowest-order predicative system by the direct application; whether it is derivable in the matrix-style predicative fragment is not proved either way (test F4).
- *Styles agree under reducibility.* `styles_equiv` (`PM/StylesEquiv.lean`): with ✱12·1 and ✱12·11, any two styles derive exactly the same theorems, with or without comprehension. It is a kernel-checked induction over derivations, uses no axioms, and uses ✱12·1/✱12·11 (it takes `[Reducibility S]`; RedCheck shows it reaches the rule). Without reducibility the matrix style admits less; that it then proves strictly less is not proved here (it needs a model that sees orders).
- *Partial application and p. 56's second clause* (`PM/Pred/Curry.lean`). `curry_order`: the order of a body as a function of two arguments is the larger of the first argument's lowest order and the order of the partial application with that argument fixed; so φ!x is of order 2 as a function of (φ, x) (pp. 54, 170) and predicative as a function of x with φ fixed (p. 55). p. 56's second clause ("if there is one of its arguments such that, when the other arguments have values assigned to them, we obtain a predicative function of the one undetermined argument") agrees with the lowest-order rule within this encoding (`second_clause_iff_lowestOrder`, and for any number of arguments `anyLe_iff_le_maxL`): "assigning values" to the other arguments is evaluating both partial applications at `()` in the one-element family `U`, so both have the same body order O, and the equivalence reduces to the arithmetic O ≤ a + 1 ∨ O ≤ b + 1 ↔ O ≤ max a b + 1. `ClauseSearch.lean` compares the same definitions on 2,784,780 cases and finds no disagreement. This does not independently show that the encoding captures PM's "assignment of values", nor extend to arbitrary variable families.
- `PM/Star12.lean`: ✱12·1 and ✱12·11, stated with PM's macros, and a check that the variable form of ✱12·1 plus comprehension gives the formula form without `red1`.
- `PM/Star13.lean`: identity. ✱13·01–✱13·03 are macros. All 25 numbered propositions ✱13·1–✱13·3 are proved. ✱13·101 follows PM's proof through ✱12·1. ✱13·3 (p. 180) is about significance; its proof's two uses of ✱10·221 identify the types of two variables, which here is the Lean typing (x and a have one type).
- `PM/Star14.lean`: descriptions. ✱14·01–✱14·04 are macros that take the descriptions' scopes explicitly. PM's conventions are applied when a statement is written: an unmarked description has the smallest scope, and of two descriptions the one written first has the larger (p. 182). All 47 numbered propositions ✱14·1–✱14·34 are proved; ✱14·17 and ✱14·171 cite ✱12·1, as PM's proofs do. ✱14·3 (p. 194) is proved as a schema, not as printed: it takes propositions p, q as apparent variables (and f is a function of propositions), and these types have none (p. 172). `q14_3` takes its antecedent p ≡ q . ⊃_{p,q} . f(p) ≡ f(q) with p, q real, as a premise proved for every p, q, so it is one theorem per f, φ, χ, not the quantified proposition; `qtfnCongr` proves the premise for every truth-function built by ✱1–✱11's processes (p. 193). PM itself proves ✱14·31–✱14·34 without ✱14·3, and so do the proofs here. ✱14·32 is proved as printed, with "≡" after E!(ιx)(φx): its hint gives the "⊃" half, and the converse, which pp. 194–195 explain, takes ✱14·21 for both descriptions (✱3·27, ✱2·54, ✱3·44; recorded in the ledger). Where the print is unclear the docstring says so: ✱14·144 and ✱14·145 cite ✱14·111 where the unmarked form is ✱14·112; ✱14·272 cites ✱10·414 for a ✱10·271 step; ✱14·16 and ✱14·203 cite "✱10·1·23" and "✱10·1·28" for ✱10·11·23 and ✱10·11·28; ✱14·28's Dem. eliminates (ιx)(φx) = (ιx)(φx) as one description. The file also proves that when the negation has the wider scope (the description the narrower), ∼{[(ιx)(φx)] . χ(ιx)(φx)} does not imply E!(ιx)(φx).
- `PM/Star20.lean`: classes. ✱20·01, ✱20·02, ✱20·04–✱20·072 are macros; identity of classes is ✱13·01 at the type of predicative functions, so ✱13 is used there at a higher type (typical ambiguity as Lean parameters). A class is written by its defining function, a real Greek letter by any formula with a hole (p. 203), an apparent one by φ!ẑ with φ bound (✱20·07, p. 198); each occurrence of a class is eliminated where it stands, with ✱14's scope conventions (p. 197) and descriptions wider than classes (p. 204). 51 of the 55 numbered propositions ✱20·1–✱20·81 are proved, ✱20·62 and ✱20·633 as rules. ✱20·631 and ✱20·632 concern significance and are built into the syntax, like ✱10·121 and ✱10·122. ✱20·4 and ✱20·41 (α ∈ Cls) are not formalized: they need ✱20·03 and the classes of classes of ✱20·08·081, which wait on a design decision. Where PM instantiates an apparent class at a class ẑ(φz) that need not be predicative, citing ✱10·1 or ✱10·24 (✱20·25, ✱20·53, ✱20·54), the step is the class analogue p. 206 points to (✱20·17's argument: ✱12·1 and ✱20·11). ✱20·59's Dem. eliminates the class before the description, against p. 204's convention; the statement follows the convention (docstring).
- `PM/Star21.lean`: relations, as ✱20 for classes ("exactly analogous … merely substituting ✱12·11 for ✱12·1, and propositions in ✱11 for propositions in ✱10", p. 212). ✱21·01, ✱21·02, ✱21·07–✱21·072 are macros. 44 of the 48 numbered propositions ✱21·1–✱21·71 are proved, ✱21·62 and ✱21·633 as rules; ✱21·631, ✱21·632 are built into the syntax; ✱21·4 and ✱21·41 (R ∈ Rel) wait on the same decision as ✱20·4·41. ✱21·57 cites ✱13·183, whose analogue for relations is not printed in ✱21; it is proved by ✱20·25's argument.
- `PM/Trap.lean`: two degenerate variable families at which every formula is a theorem (see the scope note), with ✱12·1, and at the one-element family also with comprehension alone (`trap_unit_compr`).

`AxiomCheck.lean` (generated by `data/gen_axiomcheck.py`) prints the axioms of a selection of 796 declarations: the 188 ✱2–✱5 theorems, `complete`, and the theorems and models of the ✱9–✱21 files, the rule, style and model modules. 792 report "does not depend on any axioms". The other 4 belong to the general classical collapse model (see Status). `AllAxioms.lean` checks every declaration under `PM`. A clean `lake build` (35 jobs) takes about 15 s.

**Scope of the ✱9–✱20 semantics.**

- *What the models show, and what they do not.* `qsound` holds in every structure (`QModel`), for every system. Two kinds of structure forget orders: the collapse model (`PM/Models/Collapse.lean`, every type of functions a full function space, classical quantifiers) and the finite coded model (`PM/Models/Finite.lean`, truth tables, quantifiers by enumeration). With reducibility the collapse model is a model of the 1910 system. `consistent` is proved in the finite model, so it shows that `QThm`, with ✱12·1, ✱12·11 and comprehension, does not prove everything, relative to Lean's type theory with no axioms. Neither model can see orders: a wrong order condition would still be "sound". So the order discipline is checked only syntactically (`PM/Orders.lean`), and nothing here proves that comprehension without ✱12·1 is weaker. The model is extensional, so it validates sentences that are true only extensionally. This does not affect soundness, but a counter-model can never refute a sentence that is false only intensionally.
- *Consistency is stated at a model's variables.* `QThm` is defined for every family `V`. At a family whose types are not full ranges, ✱10·11 says too much, and ✱12·1 then proves everything. This happens when a function type is empty, and also when every type has exactly one variable (`PM/Trap.lean`), so requiring non-empty types would not help. At the one-element family comprehension alone does the same as ✱12·1 (`trap_unit_compr`), so the limitation also affects the predicative fragment. Every PM theorem here is a Lean theorem for every `V`, so it holds at every model's variables. `consistent` names the variables of a model (`twoModel.den`), and `consistent_all` says that the theorems holding at every `V` do not include `p`. `QThm` at an arbitrary `V` is not PM's system.
- *Exotic terms.* `QF V` uses functions `V τ → QF V` for φx̂ (higher-order abstract syntax). Lean allows such functions to inspect their argument, so `QF V` also contains "exotic" terms that correspond to no finite formula of PM. `qsound` holds for all of them in every model, so nothing false becomes provable. But there is no adequacy theorem: no translation shows that `QF`/`QThm` correspond exactly to PM's finite formulas and derivations. The theorems are stated for arbitrary φ, so they also hold for these terms.
- *Not formalized as printed:* ✱14·3, proved as a schema (its propositions as apparent variables need a type of propositions; docs/TYPES_DESIGN.md §5, Q8; NEEDS WILL: a type of propositions).
- *Not formalized:* ✱20·03, ✱20·08, ✱20·081 and ✱20·4, ✱20·41 (classes of classes; docs/TYPES_DESIGN.md §5, Q6; NEEDS WILL: classes of classes (✱20·08 etc.)); ✱21·03, ✱21·08–✱21·083 and ✱21·4, ✱21·41 (relations of relations, classes of relations); ✱22 onward; functions of three or more arguments; the 1925 second edition's system.

## Building and checking

Requires the Lean toolchain named in `lean-toolchain` (via [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan)); no other dependencies.

```
lake build                      # builds every file
lake env lean AxiomCheck.lean   # 796 selected declarations; 792 report "does not depend on any axioms"
lake env lean AllAxioms.lean    # every declaration under PM, private ones included; lists the few that use an axiom
lake env lean RedCheck.lean     # which declarations use ✱12·1 / ✱12·11 or comprehension; cross-check with their signatures (fails unless it flags its explicit-evidence test)
lake env lean StyleCheck.lean   # every PM proposition instantiated under every style and named system
lake env lean ClauseSearch.lean # p. 56's second clause vs the lowest-order rule (about 1 min)
```
